Dr. Arulmurugan Ramu

I am an Associate Professor in the Department of Computational Science and Software Engineering at Heriot-Watt University – Aktobe Campus, Kazakhstan, specialising in Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, and Deep Learning. My research focuses on developing intelligent systems that solve real-world problems in healthcare, computer vision, and natural language understanding. I lead the AI & Intelligent Systems Lab and collaborate with international research groups across academia and industry.

Research

My lab investigates foundational and applied problems in AI, with a focus on building systems that are accurate, interpretable, and clinically deployable.

Medical Image Analysis

Deep learning models for early detection and classification of diseases from MRI, CT, and X-ray images. Current focus on brain tumour segmentation and diabetic retinopathy grading.

CNN U-Net Transfer Learning

Natural Language Processing

Transformer-based models for text classification, sentiment analysis, and information extraction from biomedical literature and clinical notes.

Transformers BERT Text Mining

Explainable AI

Developing interpretability methods for black-box models to improve transparency and trust in AI-driven clinical decision support systems.

Federated Learning

Privacy-preserving distributed machine learning frameworks enabling collaborative model training across hospitals and institutions without sharing raw patient data.

Research Vision

Bridging artificial intelligence and real-world impact — in healthcare, language, and society.

My research sits at the intersection of machine learning, natural language processing, and clinical decision support. I am driven by a single question: how can AI systems be made not only accurate, but trustworthy and deployable in high-stakes environments?

Over the past decade, my work has focused on three converging frontiers. First, medical image analysis — developing deep learning pipelines for early detection of brain tumours, diabetic retinopathy, and skin lesions from MRI, CT, and dermatoscopic images. Second, low-resource NLP — building text classification, named entity recognition, and speech systems for under-resourced languages such as Afaan-Oromo, Tamil, and Arabic, where data scarcity demands creative transfer learning strategies. Third, explainable AI (XAI) — designing interpretability layers using SHAP, Grad-CAM, and attention mechanisms so that clinical professionals can understand, audit, and safely act on model predictions.

Looking ahead to 2025–2030, my lab is expanding into federated learning for privacy-preserving clinical collaboration, LLM fine-tuning for biomedical question answering, and edge-deployable AI for resource-constrained healthcare settings in Central Asia and East Africa. The goal is not publication counts — it is measurable benefit to patients, practitioners, and communities.
